Triunfo YMCA Baseball Fields

A prime example of our capabilities to visualize and deliver a community project — worked directly with the YMCA.

The Triunfo YMCA baseball fields are the job CPF points at when a client asks whether a residential mason can handle something larger. It is a community facility, used constantly, built to be walked on by hundreds of people a week — and the masonry has to survive that without a maintenance budget behind it.

CPF worked directly with the YMCA on the build: brick backstop walls, dugouts, block retaining structures around the outfield, and the concrete flatwork — walkways, steps and pads — that ties the whole facility together and makes it accessible.

What the job involved

  • Brick backstop walls — the structure carrying the chain-link behind home plate, taking impact for the life of the field.
  • Dugouts — poured footings, brick base courses, and perforated screens over bench seating.
  • Block outfield walls — curved segmental retaining walls following the boundary grade.
  • Concrete flatwork — spectator walkways, steps and equipment pads.

Why it matters for a residential client

Institutional work is awarded on scheduling and insurance as much as on the masonry itself. A community organization cannot have a field out of use into the season, which is the same reason a homeowner cannot have a driveway open for a month. The guaranteed completion date is not a marketing line here — it is the condition the job was won on.

Structures on the field

  • Red brick backstop wall carrying the chain-link behind home plate.

  • Curved segmental block retaining wall along the outfield boundary.

  • Equipment shed and enclosure at the edge of the field.
